🧠 Your Reading Detective Toolkit

🔍 Strategy 1: Central Idea + Evidence

Central idea is what an entire informational passage is mostly teaching. Supporting evidence is a detail that proves or develops that idea. Ask: “Which idea connects the most important details?”

Example: “Students planted native flowers. Butterflies soon visited. The garden also attracted bees.”

Central idea: Planting native flowers helps pollinators. Evidence: Butterflies and bees visited the garden.

🕵️ Strategy 2: Inference

An inference combines what the text says + what you already know. Don't guess beyond the clues.

Example: “Jada zipped her jacket, tightened her scarf, and hurried through the frosty doorway.” You can infer that the weather is cold.

🧩 Strategy 3: Context Clues and Purpose

To understand an unfamiliar word, read before and after the word. To identify an author's purpose, ask whether the writer mainly wants to inform, explain, persuade, or entertain. Notice how the passage is organized: sequence, cause/effect, compare/contrast, problem/solution, or description.
